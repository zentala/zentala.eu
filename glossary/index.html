Glossary — 19 terms

Every governed term on this site, in one place. Each is defined once and linked from wherever it is used first on a page.

Middle — the digital state

The digital state Defined

A state that touches everything, sees everything and can act everywhere at once — total in reach, not totalitarian.

Named positively on `/vision` (the middle layer) and as a warning in the manifesto: the same system. See [The Total State](/book/the-total-state#why-a-digital-state-is-a-total-state).

Ceiling — robotics

European Health Data Space Defined outside this site

A legal route for reusing health data across borders for research and policy, through data access bodies, permits and secure processing environments, on top of GDPR. Named once in passing at [European Science](/book/european-science); not defined in Paweł's own words on this site.

How we get there

Cheap as the goal Defined

Wealth is when things are cheap, not when they are expensive; making things cheap is the goal, not a side effect.

See [Cheap Is Wealth](/book/cheap-is-wealth) for the full argument.

Citizens' initiative Defined

Citizens collect signatures to propose a change and force a national vote on it — the accelerator.

In Switzerland: 100,000 signatures within 18 months, for a change to the federal constitution. See [Direct Democracy](/book/direct-democracy#the-words-in-one-place).

Read where it is argued →

Citizens' wealth fund Defined

Defined by example: Norway's Government Pension Fund Global, the largest sovereign wealth fund in the world, built from petroleum revenue.

Alaska did something different with the same idea: an annual cash dividend to every resident. See [Who It Earns For](/book/who-it-earns-for#words-that-fit-better). One abstract sentence still wanted (IA §3).

Double majority Defined

A proposal passes only with a majority of the votes cast and a majority of the cantons or member states.

Applies to constitutional change and to joining supranational communities, not to the ordinary citizen veto. Worked mechanics: [The Safeguards](/book/safeguards#the-safeguards-one-by-one).

European Citizens' Initiative Defined

One million signatures from seven member states within twelve months puts a demand on the Commission's desk; it binds nobody.

The Union's existing instrument — a citizens' initiative in name, with no binding force. See [First Move](/book/first-move#how-it-actually-works).

Optional referendum Defined

Citizens collect signatures to put a law parliament has already passed to a national vote that can strike it down — the brake.

In Switzerland: 50,000 voters or eight cantons within 100 days of publication. See [Direct Democracy](/book/direct-democracy#the-words-in-one-place).

Requisite variety Defined

A controller must have at least as much variety in its responses as the system it steers throws at it — only variety can absorb variety.

Ross Ashby's law; Stafford Beer applied it to organisations and states. The definition itself is Ashby's — attribution to Paweł's voice is an open question (IA §3). See [Steering](/book/steering#a-controller-must-correct-at-least-as-fast-as-the-thing-it-steers).

Semi-direct democracy Defined

Parliament and government carry on; citizens hold two instruments — to propose a change and to strike one down.

The system Switzerland runs and this site proposes: nothing abolished, something added. See [Direct Democracy](/book/direct-democracy#the-words-in-one-place) for the two instruments this adds and how they work.
