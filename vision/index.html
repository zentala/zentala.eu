The frame

We are an aging society. We can design what comes next.

  1. 1 · The shared condition

    Europe is aging. Birth rates are below replacement in every member state. This is not a national problem with national fixes; it is the same problem everywhere, which makes it the strongest argument for acting together — and preparing for a future without generational replacement is one of the most important decisions our societies and economies face.

  2. 2 · The consequence, and the official answer

    Pensions and public services rest on growth plus generational replacement. Both end within roughly thirty years, and governments already say so: they warn of an economic crisis and of changes that could shake the economy. Their answer is to bring in migrants, so that someone is left to do the work.

  3. 3 · That is not the only answer

    Those warnings show the problem is real. They do not show that importing workers is the only way to deal with it. Money is a claim on someone else’s labour, and that labour comes from people or from machines. There will not be enough people.

  4. 4 · The window, and the goal

    We are not old yet. We have roughly thirty years of working economy left to build what will carry us; this is the last generation that can pay for it, and the technology arrived in time. So name the goal: a robotic economy that pays for our retirement. Machines that do the work there will not be enough people to do.

So the plan is to shift our expectations onto robotics, deliberately, as a strategy, not as a hope. Robotics needs the layers beneath it. Build them in order.

Three layers, in build order

Not three parallel pillars. Each level is what makes the next one possible, and the ownership question runs through all of them.

  1. 1

    Level 1 · Floor

    One economy, one working language

    A shrinking workforce has to be organised well, because there is no slack left. English as the official working language of European business and administration is the precondition.

    • ▸ English is the bottleneck of the European information economy: the most capable people cannot work with each other across borders without it.
    • ▸ No common language is the likely reason Europe has produced no startups or corporations of European scale, ones whose capital cannot be called French, German or Dutch.
    • ▸ A shared working language produces a European political consciousness, not only a business one.
    • ▸ A shared interest is what keeps us from the next war. The Union was built so there would be no more war; this is the mechanism for this century.

  2. 2

    Level 2 · Middle

    The digital state

    With a common working language the state itself can be rebuilt: digital administration, digital public services, and direct democracy in place of representation that serves lobbies.

    • ▸ Digital administration and public services cut the cost of running a state that will have fewer taxpayers.
    • ▸ Direct democracy is the route out of dependence on a political class we did not choose and cannot correct between elections.
    • ▸ Regulation becomes a tool for improving how the state works, not a substitute for it.

  3. 3

    Level 3 · Ceiling

    Robotics and autonomous systems

    On top of a digital state, automation becomes possible at scale. The goal, stated plainly: automate as much of old-age provision as possible, so the few young people left are not crushed carrying it.

    • ▸ Communal and municipal services: the most mechanical, the most staffed, the first to lose workers.
    • ▸ Food production and delivery: autonomous farming and logistics.
    • ▸ Predictive medicine and healthcare: the cost that grows fastest as a population ages.
    • ▸ Military robotics research funded by the war with Russia can be converted to civilian use afterwards. This is the realistic route to funding robotics at European scale.

Runs through every layer

Who captures the gains?

If automation replaces the labour our pensions depend on and the returns go to a handful of owners, we have solved production and destroyed the distribution that made it worth solving.

Ownership will be mixed: state, cooperative, private, dividend. Who owns the robots matters less than one thing: robots must not be expensive.

  • ✓ Cheap to buy and run
  • ✓ Available to every municipality, farm and clinic
  • ✓ Built on open standards
  • ✓ Hard to monopolise

Pick a layer and carry it.

This is a plan for the thirty working years Europe has left, written to be carried rather than admired: each of the three layers above is something a reader can argue for, vote for, or build.

Every person who repeats it, argues it, or builds a piece of it moves it closer. The manifesto says what that asks of you; the chapters say what to build, and in what order.
