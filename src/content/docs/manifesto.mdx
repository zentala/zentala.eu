## I am a positivist, and complaint was all I could find

I built this site because I was angry, and not at the government.

I was angry at the conversation around me in Poland. This does not work. That
does not work. The politics is broken, the state is broken, nothing can be
done. Everywhere I looked I found people naming what is wrong and stopping
there. I do not think they are wrong about the facts. I think they stopped one
step too early.

Seeing a problem is not the same as being unable to act. That distinction is
the whole reason this site exists. So instead of adding another voice to the
complaint, I started asking a different question: what could actually be done?
Not what is unacceptable — what is worth building.

## The bet I am making

Here is the bet, stated plainly so you can judge it.

If enough clear visions of what is genuinely worth building are put in front of
people — not one idea, a programme of several hundred positive, constructive
changes that support one another, well ahead of anything the current
administration is proposing — then people start to want those things. And once
they want them, they demand them.

The mechanism is attention. Today our attention sits on what we do not have and
on who is to blame for it. Move it onto what we could have, and two things
follow. A social expectation forms, which is the only thing politics reliably
answers to. And with it comes the awareness that these things are achievable —
that the gap between now and them is work, not fate.

That is the bet. It may be wrong. I would rather test it than keep reading the
complaint.

## Know what you want

This is the demand the whole site rests on, and it is aimed at us, not at them.

<ManifestoDemand label="The first demand">
  Know what you want. Being disappointed in your government and complaining that
  things are bad, without knowing what you want instead, is the most dangerous
  position you can hold.
</ManifestoDemand>

It is dangerous because the space does not stay empty. If we do not define our
interest, other interest groups will define theirs, and they will hand the
government their proposals — written, costed, ready to sign. Those proposals
will not be ours. That is not a conspiracy; it is simply what happens when one
side turns up with a programme and the other turns up with a grievance.

We are the sovereign. It falls to us to say what is in our interest, and
capital will arrange itself around that — it always does. But it can only
arrange itself around something that exists.

Ten years ago we did not have this conversation, and maybe we should have.
Maybe English should have become an official working language of the Union
after the crisis of 2008. Maybe we should have built the railways then, while
the workforce was still young enough to build them. I do not know that those
were the right calls. I know we never made them, because we never discussed
them — and so we cannot now demand what was never asked for.

And I notice what the surplus of human capital is being pointed at instead. We
are being organised toward war rather than toward building. Toward destruction
rather than construction. That is a choice somebody is making while we argue
about the last press conference.

The same question, brought close to home: what do we actually want our
retirement to look like? The pension system is widely expected to buckle under
the demographic shift. Do we have a plan? Robots, we say — and I agree, that is
the answer. But robots require political stability, AI centres, microchips,
rare metals bought on a world market, and the money to buy them. None of that
assembles itself. We have to know what we are building and how we intend to get
there, and we have to insist that the gains from that transformation reach
citizens rather than stopping short of them.

## The work is going away, and that is the time we need

Artificial intelligence and a crisis that has been building for years are going
to take a great deal of work away from us. Programmers in Poland are already
finding that out.

I know how that sounds coming from me, so here is where I am writing from. I
have not worked for seven months. I spend my days coding things with AI and
writing down what I think, including this. I had started this piece perhaps
eight times over the years and abandoned it every time. I would not have
finished it without AI. That is not a complaint and it is not a success story —
it is the evidence I have that the freed time is real and that it can be spent
on something.

That is the part nobody is saying out loud. If a lot of people are about to have
no work, they will have a great deal of time. Time to do politics. Time to build
the state. The question is not whether the time appears; it is whether anyone
offers those people something worth doing with it.

<ManifestoDemand label="The demand">
  Print the money to pay people to build the robotics and the digital state.
</ManifestoDemand>

We print money already and we spend enormous sums on a long list of other
things. This is our future. It is absurd that we are not investing in it. I know
this is the demand in here that economists will argue with hardest, and I am not
going to pretend I can settle that argument. I am stating it as what I want:
pay people to build the machines that will carry our retirement and the systems
that will run our state, rather than paying for everything except that.

Because there is another use for people with nothing to do, and states have
reached for it before. No work, no pensions to pay, a crisis nobody can explain
— and then a war, which explains everything afterwards. I am not predicting it.
I am saying we have a choice between sending those people to kill each other and
sending them to build the things we actually need, and that almost nobody is
doing the work of defining what we need.

## We get as much as we expect

I think politicians give us as much as we expect of them, and no more. If we
allow ourselves to expect little, little is what we will be handed. Raising the
standard of what the public expects from the state is part of this manifesto,
not a preface to it. I would like it to become obvious that a state should be
building its own future, the way it is obvious that it should collect the bins.

Nor should we expect business to raise it for us. Large change is not in its
interest, and this is a mechanism rather than a plot. If I own a railway, I want
the old railway to last as long as possible. If the railway belongs to the
state, what I want is to drain it slowly. There is a long list of such
arrangements, and together they are why the state does not work the way we want
and why we have so little say in it. That say is what has to be taken back.

## Why there is no European artificial intelligence

I think the answer is simpler than the usual one about funding.

There is no European artificial intelligence because there is no European
science and because we do not work in one language. Real collaboration exists in
a few places — theoretical physics has it — but in the practical fields that
decide the next thirty years, AI and robotics, there is no room in Europe where
the best people from every country sit shoulder to shoulder and work. There are
visits and programmes. That is not the same thing.

So the language question sits upstream of the science question, and the science
question sits upstream of European business. If we want European AI, the first
thing to fix is that our ablest people cannot work together without changing
country and language first.

## Look for the place where one plus one makes three

I want to turn attention away from anger at politicians and away from the
feeling of helplessness, and onto what is good. Onto cooperation — not polite
cooperation, the deep kind, where you look for the arrangement in which both
sides genuinely gain. The space where one plus one makes three.

I once had the thought that love is the feeling with which you naturally find
the place where both of you win. I am not claiming that as an objective
statement about the world. I am saying that looking for that place is a skill,
and that we spend almost no time practising it in public life.

The alternatives currently on offer are aggression toward migrants or
aggression toward politicians. I understand where both come from. Neither
builds anything. This site is the other route, and I am not aware of a third.

## A digital state is a total state

There is one reform I want more than the others, and it is not the exciting one.

The more digital the state becomes, the more total it becomes. That is not a
slogan — it is the plain mechanics of digital identity and digital money. What
can be switched on for you can be switched off. This is why people are
protesting against these systems, and the protests are not irrational.

I am not against digitalising the state. I think the potential is enormous, and
I think Central Europe should build it. But I will not hand a government more
power over me while I have no more power over it. So the two have to move
together: as the state goes digital, citizens' control over the state has to
grow with it. To strengthen the Union we have to strengthen the citizen's grip
on the institutions — otherwise people will refuse the whole thing, and they
will be right to.

The two halves of that argument have their own chapters: [the total
state](/book/the-total-state) for the danger, and [direct
democracy](/book/direct-democracy) for the counterweight.

## What I do not know

I have been writing these reform visions since roughly 2016, and possibly
earlier — I would have to look at when the repository actually starts. It took
a long time to put down.

I do not know whether any of it matters. That is the honest state of it. I want
to find out whether a word and a thought can change anything at all. Maybe they
can. Maybe a vision that people can agree on is enough to keep the Union from
falling apart and to give us back some say over our own future. Maybe not. I am
writing here to find out, and I would rather say that than pretend to a
certainty I do not have.

## So — how would you want to live?

That is the question I actually want to ask you, and I want it answered rather
than admired.

<ManifestoDemand label="The question">
  What kind of state would you want? How would you want to live? What would you
  be willing to give yourself to?
</ManifestoDemand>

Nothing in this programme needs permission to begin. It needs enough people to
want it that wanting it becomes a fact politicians have to answer to. That is
what critical mass means here, and it is the only lever ordinary people have
ever had.

Which makes you the mechanism, not the audience. If you argue this, repeat it,
send it to someone who would disagree with it, or build one piece of it — the
odds of it happening go up. That is the only way such things have ever
happened.
