The timing of this proposal is not rhetorical. The numbers force it.

By 2050, the European old-age dependency ratio reaches roughly two workers per pensioner — down from four in 2010 (Eurostat projections). The Draghi Report (September 2024) concluded that without a significant productivity boost the EU's GDP will shrink in absolute terms before 2050. The post-1945 international order that protected European prosperity is visibly thinning: the United States is renegotiating its commitments, Russia is testing borders, and the institutions that were supposed to backstop us are slower than the threats they face.

Across Europe, public confidence in the existing model of integration is falling. In several member states, parties that openly question the current direction now hold parliamentary majorities that would have been unthinkable a decade ago. This is not a fringe phenomenon — it is the median European voter telling Brussels that the status quo is not working and that something has to change. The choice in the next decade is not between reform and stability. It is between reform and a different reform we will not choose.

I do not think this calls for panic. I think it calls for the opposite: the patient, deliberate building of the infrastructure that lets Europe survive the next twenty years on its own terms — common digital backbone, common robotic capacity, common working language, common defensive technology. Each of these takes time. The longer we wait, the more of that time we lose.

**The reason to act now is not catastrophe. It is that the systems we want to build take ten to twenty years to build, and we have already used some of that time.**
