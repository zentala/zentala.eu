Two videos from the channel Good Times Bad Times argue that the United States is
deprioritizing European security while Russia keeps fighting despite its losses.
Europe, the videos say, has under-invested in Ukraine's defense (around 0.3% of
GDP) and has no plan for American disengagement. One proposes a "Baltic-Carpathian
Alliance" (Intermarium 2.0) of Poland, Romania, Sweden, Finland and possibly the
UK, Czech Republic and Slovakia, with member defense spending of at least 3% of
GDP, running alongside NATO and the EU as a regional counterweight to Russia. The
other argues Europe should take over control of Ukrainian airspace and war
leadership directly, rather than leaving that role to the US.

**My comment.** Both videos point at the same gap: Europe has treated American
protection as a given for so long that it never built the capacity to act without
it. That assumption is now wrong, and the cost of being wrong compounds every
year Europe spends 0.3% of GDP instead of 3%.

A regional alliance among the states that actually border the threat makes more
sense than waiting for EU-27 consensus on every defense decision. It will not
replace NATO, but it does not need to — it needs to make sure the frontline states
can act even when Washington and Brussels are slow. Taking over Ukrainian
airspace is a bigger step, and probably premature without the alliance's
industrial base behind it first. But the direction is right: Europe stops asking
who will protect it and starts building that capacity itself.
